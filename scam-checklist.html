Student safety

The internship scam checklist.

Every year, students lose money to fake internships. Most scams use the same playbook. Learn it once, and you'll spot it forever.

8 red flags5 verification stepsFree

Red flags

If you see any of these, stop.

One red flag is a warning. Two is a pattern. Walk away and tell someone.

01

Red flag

They ask you to pay to get the internship

“Registration fee”, “training deposit”, “verification charge”, “refundable security amount” — any upfront payment for the privilege of working is the single biggest red flag. Real employers pay you; you don't pay them.

02

Red flag

“Guaranteed placement” or “100% internship assurance”

No legitimate company can guarantee outcomes before evaluating you. Guarantees are marketing for a trap — often paired with an upfront fee.

03

Red flag

No verifiable company behind the offer

Check: does the company have a real website (not just Instagram), a registered address, real employees on LinkedIn, a GSTIN or MCA listing? If you can't verify the company exists, walk away.

04

Red flag

The interview happens only on chat, in a hurry

Pressure tactics — “offer expires today”, interviews entirely over WhatsApp/Telegram with no video call, no written offer letter. Legitimate hiring has a paper trail.

05

Red flag

They ask for sensitive documents too early

Aadhaar, PAN, bank details, or OTPs before a signed offer letter. Never share OTPs with anyone, ever. Share documents only after verifying the employer.

06

Red flag

Vague work, grand promises

“Work from home, 2 hours a day, earn ₹40,000/month, no skills needed.” If the pay is high, the work is vague, and the bar is zero — it's bait.

07

Red flag

They want you to recruit others

If the “internship” mainly involves bringing in more interns who each pay a fee, that's not an internship. That's a pyramid wearing a blazer.

08

Red flag

No written terms, ever

No offer letter, no scope of work, no stipend terms in writing, no named supervisor. If it's not written down, it doesn't exist.

Before you say yes

Verify in five steps.

Takes twenty minutes. Saves you months of regret.

  1. 01Search the company name + “fraud” / “scam” / “review”.
  2. 02Check MCA (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) or GST records for registration.
  3. 03Look up 2–3 real employees on LinkedIn — do they exist, with history?
  4. 04Insist on a written offer letter with scope, stipend, duration, supervisor.
  5. 05Never pay to start. Never share OTPs. Ever.

Our standard

Every listing we publish is verified first.

Method, verifier, and date — on every listing, or an honest empty state when there's nothing verified to show. That's the bar this checklist holds us to as well.
